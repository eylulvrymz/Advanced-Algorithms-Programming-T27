**Exercise 2:** We started by tackling the exact solution through dynamic programming — we built a 2D table where each cell tracks the best reach achievable with a given number of users and remaining budget, then traced back through it to figure out exactly which users were picked. This directly answers the professor's request for an O(N × budget) solution that works for N up to 500 and budget up to 10,000. We also wrote the small `is_within_budget` helper which just adds up the costs of a chosen group and checks if they fit, simple but necessary. For the greedy approach, we ranked users by how much reach you get per dollar spent, then kept picking the best value users until the budget ran out. Finally, we ran both methods on the same inputs to compare them, and the results clearly showed that greedy doesn't always win — in one of our tests it got a reach of 12 while the exact method found 13, and in our deliberate counterexample it only reached 7 versus the optimal 10, which is exactly the kind of comparison the professor was looking for.

**Complexity Analysis:** The DP approach works well because instead of blindly testing every possible combination of users, it breaks the problem into smaller pieces and saves the results along the way, which is what keeps it at O(N × budget). That said it has a clear breaking point — once you start talking about millions of users and billion dollar budgets the table becomes too large to even store in memory, and if costs are real numbers rather than clean integers the whole approach falls apart completely. That is where greedy comes in, it just ranks users by how much reach you get per dollar and keeps picking until the money runs out, which makes it fast and scalable no matter how large the input gets. The catch is that it is not always right, we saw this directly in our counterexample where greedy got stuck at a reach of 7 while the exact method found 10 by thinking a step ahead. In the real world though, platforms with hundreds of millions of users do not have the luxury of running exact methods, so they take the greedy answer knowing it is close enough and move on.
